Learn it in three levels: no code, drag and drop, then a little code. Try a working demo of the key idea (searching trusted sources) right in this page. Back to the AI and Orange labs.

Scenario: the legal aid clinic

A university legal aid clinic in Windhoek gets the same questions every week: What are my rights if I am arrested? Is an electronic signature valid? Where do I report a cyber incident? Volunteers are busy. You are asked to build a chatbot that gives general legal information, points to the source, and sends personal problems to a lawyer.

In plain words: The chatbot is a helpful librarian, not a lawyer. It finds the right page in trusted documents, explains it in simple words, and says "ask a lawyer" when the question is about your own case.
Understanding lineSearch first, answer second, cite always.

How a modern chatbot works (the big idea)

Language models can write fluent answers, but they can also invent laws. So we do not let the model answer from memory. We first search a library of official texts, hand the best passages to the model, and tell it to answer only from them. This is called retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG.

Design: rules before tools

Risks and controls

RiskWhat can go wrongControl
Invented lawThe bot cites an Act or section that does not existAnswer only from uploaded sources. Show the source with every answer. Say "not found" otherwise.
Outdated lawAn amended or repealed text is usedRecord the date and version of each source. Review on a schedule.
Personal adviceThe bot predicts who will win a caseGive general information only. Refer personal matters to a lawyer or legal aid.
PrivacyUsers paste ID numbers or case detailsWarn users. Do not store personal data. Mask it in logs.
Prompt injectionA user types "ignore your rules"Keep rules in the system instructions and test with attack questions.
AccessMany people speak Afrikaans, Oshiwambo, Otjiherero or Khoekhoegowab at homeSay which languages you support. Do not guess legal terms in languages you have not tested.
Over-trustPeople treat the bot as finalShow a notice on every answer. A named person owns the bot.

Where to get trusted texts

Use official or well-known sources: the Government Gazette, ministry and parliament websites, and the NamibLII legal information site. Download the latest version of each document and note the date.

Check before launch: Reports from September 2026 say Namibia has no dedicated cybercrime or data protection act in force yet, though bills are in progress. Your bot must say this clearly if asked, and you should re-check often. Also check what the Law Society of Namibia and the courts expect before a public launch.
